Floods & Rising Waters

During Rising Water:
  • Move immediately to higher ground or upper building floors.
  • Turn off main power switches and gas cylinders before water enters the premises.
  • Disconnect inverter batteries if water is rising near electrical outlets.
  • Never step into flowing water. Just 6 inches of fast water can knock a person over.
Trapped in a Building:
  • Climb to the roof only if necessary; signal rescue teams with a bright cloth or whistle.
  • Do NOT hide in a closed attic without an exit to the roof; you risk becoming trapped.
  • Boil all drinking water or use chlorine tablets. Floodwaters carry severe contaminants.

Earthquakes & Structural Tremors

Drop, Cover, and Hold On:
  • DROP down onto your hands and knees.
  • COVER your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk.
  • HOLD ON to your shelter until shaking stops.
  • Stay away from glass windows, exterior doors, and heavy furniture.
Trapped Under Debris:
  • Cover your mouth with a cloth or clothing to avoid suffocating on dust.
  • Do NOT light matches or lighters (risk of broken gas pipelines).
  • Tap on a pipe or wall at regular intervals so rescuers can triangulate your location.
  • Shout only as a last resort to preserve energy and prevent dust inhalation.

Fires & Smoke Inhalation

Evacuation Protocol:
  • Stay low to the floor: Smoke and lethal carbon monoxide rise upwards.
  • Check door handles with the back of your hand. If hot, do NOT open.
  • NEVER use elevators during a fire. Use staircases only.
  • If your clothes catch fire: STOP, DROP, and ROLL.
If Trapped by Flames:
  • Close doors between you and the fire.
  • Seal cracks around doors with wet towels or bedsheets.
  • Signal through an open window with a bright cloth or flashlight.

Urgent First Aid Protocols

Severe Bleeding:

Apply firm, continuous pressure directly over the wound with a clean cloth. Elevate the wounded limb above heart level if no bone fracture is suspected. Do NOT remove soaked cloths; place additional cloths on top.

Burns:

Cool the burn under cool, clean running water for at least 15 minutes. Never apply ice, butter, or paste. Cover loosely with sterile non-stick bandage.

Unconscious but Breathing:

Place the victim into the Recovery Position (turn gently onto their side, tilt head backwards to keep airway open, bend upper leg at right angle). Monitor breathing continuously.

Suspected Spinal / Head Injury:

Keep the patient completely still. Do NOT twist or bend the neck or spine unless in imminent danger of explosion or fire.

72-Hour Emergency Survival Go-Bag

3 Litres drinking water per person per day
Non-perishable, ready-to-eat dry rations (dates, nuts, biscuits)
Battery-powered or hand-crank radio & flashlight
Essential prescription medicines (7-day supply)
First aid kit (gauze, antiseptic, triangular bandages)
Emergency whistle to signal responders
Power bank with USB cable (charged)
Waterproof pouch with identity cards & cash
